How it holds you up when you sit

No counterweight. Anything stays up when its weight sits above what it stands on. The green or red dot is your weight; the dashed line drops straight down from it.
Left, why it fails: with only your shoes touching the ground, the base is just heel to toe. Sitting back puts your weight behind your heel, so you go over backward. The only way to stay up is to lean way forward and bend your ankles past what's comfortable, which is just a squat. You were right.
Right, why it works: a leg folds down behind each thigh to the ground, about 15 in behind your heel. Now each side stands on three points: heel, toes and rear foot. Your weight falls inside that base with about 11 in to spare behind you, so it sits like a stool. The pads carry you, the knee lock and the rear legs carry the pads, and your muscles rest.
