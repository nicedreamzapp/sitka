PERCH IS A LEAN-ASSIST, NOT HANDS-FREE SITTING (firmware, app and manual must never call it hands-free): the knee lock takes the bend, but your own calves keep you balanced,
exactly as when standing. Backward margin 5 cm (3° tilt). Lean on a wall or pole, or deploy the rear legs and use Half-sit.
Perch has no rear leg: the wearer balances at the ankle exactly as when standing, 2 Nm per ankle (calf), CoM -0.1 cm behind the ankle.

Firmware rule: with no rear leg detected on the ground (foot switch), ONLY Perch may lock.

=== WHY v1 HAD TO GO ===
v1 chair: shank lean 32.9° (past the ~20° comfortable dorsiflexion), pad CoP placed over the heel.
Whole-body CoM 5.4 cm from the heel edge (the BACK edge of the support polygon) → backward margin ~5 cm, a 4° backward tilt to go over.
A 3–4 cm lean back, a pack shift or a slope and you go over backward. "The feet take the rest" meant muscles,
which contradicted the 100% load share it assumed. The rear leg moves the back edge ~0.4 m further back.

=== CAN THE MOTOR HOLD IT? ===
Chair with rear leg: 79 Nm per knee. AK10-9 peak 48 Nm → 1.7x its peak, and 92 W of heat at 48 Nm. Still a mechanical lock.
v1 errors, for the record: v1 chair needed 160 Nm per knee in this model (v1 report: 142 Nm); the 48 Nm motor could never
unload a pawl carrying that to let it lift, and a small solenoid cannot pull a loaded pawl out (~2 kN tooth friction).
v2 never asks either: the ratchet lets the knee EXTEND, which unloads the pawl by itself.
(AK80-64: 120 Nm peak, but 23 rpm cannot follow a running knee — rejected, see gait_assist_model.mjs.)

=== ONE-WAY RATCHET (blocks flexion; extension always free) ===
Fallback design load (rear leg slips, thigh horizontal, all weight on ONE leg, x1.5): 507 Nm — the lock and rails are still sized for this.
v1 pawl   : 36T at r = 35 mm, 8 mm wide, 1 tooth/teeth loaded → 14.5 kN each, bending 1976 MPa vs 17-4PH H900 yield ~1170 MPa (VERIFY) → FAILS; step 10°
v2 ratchet: 40T at r = 50 mm, 12 mm wide, 2 tooth/teeth loaded → 5.1 kN each, bending 359 MPa vs 17-4PH H900 yield ~1170 MPa (VERIFY) → SF 3.3; step 9°
Single tooth (one of the two misses): 10.1 kN, bending 717 MPa → SF 1.6. Thin: PROOF TEST to 507 Nm is required.
Flank contact, MATCHED flank angles (flat-on-flat, crown relief at the edges): 134 MPa with 2 teeth, 269 MPa on one tooth.
Line contact (mismatched angles) would be far higher: the drawing must specify matched flank angles + crown relief.
DUAL PAWL offset half a pitch (4.5°): worst sag onto a tooth = 2.7 cm at the pad, 3.6 cm at the hip (single pawl: 7.2 cm).
MOUNTING: sector on the AK10-9 output flange, 8 x M4 on a 72 mm circle (EST): 1.76 kN shear each at 507 Nm
  vs ~6.4 kN for a 12.9 M4 (0.6 x 1220 MPa x 8.78 mm²) → SF 3.7. Pawl housing on the actuator case's outboard face.
Pawl-load sensor (strain gauge) and hall sensor on each pawl.

SEQUENCE (re-audited). The motor CANNOT hold any seat target: 78–94 Nm per knee with the rear leg, 145–190 without, vs 48 Nm peak.
  1 DROP: your QUADS hold you, the motor adds braking. Pawls held UP (solenoid + detent).
  2 SEAT: at the target the pawls are released; you LOWER UNTIL SEATED — hall says engaged AND load sensor carries load —
    and only then does the motor let go. Sag onto the next tooth is the half-pitch figure above.
  3 EMERGENCY CATCH: if the knee flexes unexpectedly, the pawls drop ONLY below 60°/s (threshold from the re-audit, EST: above it the
    impact on a tooth is not tested, so the catch is not attempted). The DESCENT IS UNPROTECTED except by this catch. Say it plainly in the manual.
  4 STAND: extension is always free. The pawls CLICK over the teeth on the way up: that is correct, not a fault.
    Below 20° and extending, the solenoid lifts the pawls for walking.
  5 DETENT: a sprung mechanical detent holds each pawl up/down; the bistable solenoid's 4 N alone must never be able to drop a pawl mid-stride.
  6 DEPTH CHANGE: the rear pole is locked in compression and cannot shorten, so you change depth only by STANDING (knee < 20°)
    and sitting again. Never re-seat deeper in place.
  (A small solenoid cannot drag a pawl out under ~2 kN of tooth friction, so the sequence never asks it to.)

=== REAR FOOT SLIP (friction NEEDED = tan of the leg angle) ===
Worst mode needs mu = 0.30 (design cap 0.3). Pivoting rubber foot keeps full rubber contact at any leg angle.
  rubber, dry concrete                           0.60–0.85  → holds, 2.0x margin at the low end   [SRC 17 (Engineering ToolBox)]
  rubber, wet concrete                           0.45–0.75  → holds, 1.5x margin at the low end   [SRC 17 (Engineering ToolBox)]
  rubber, wet concrete (low source)              0.30–0.30  → holds, 1.0x margin at the low end   [SRC 17 (Autodesk Robot help)]
  wet interior tile, rated                       0.42–0.42  → holds, 1.4x margin at the low end   [SRC 17 (ANSI A326.3 minimum wet DCOF)]
  wet tile below rating, wet polished stone, ice 0.00–0.30  → holds only at the upper end: not promised   [EST, unrated: no promise]
Wet-floor limit, plainly: rated wet tile (≥0.42) and wet concrete hold; wet polished stone, unrated wet tile, mud and ice do NOT.
There, flip down the spike tip (outdoors) or use Perch. The FSR + load check flags a rear foot that unloads or skids.

=== HOW MUCH OF THE KNEE CAN ONE AK10-9 CARRY? ===

-- median man, 87.4 kg --
Activity                                         bio peak   AK10-9 48 Nm peak   18 Nm rated
Level walk (peak, Winter ~0.5 Nm/kg, UNVERIFIED)    44 Nm     110 %              41 %
Stair ascent (Riener 2002 via review)               93 Nm      52 %              19 %
Stair descent (Riener 2002 via review)              80 Nm      60 %              22 %
Run 3.5 m/s, midstance (Schache 2011)              273 Nm      18 %               7 %

-- 95th pct man, 130.3 kg --
Activity                                         bio peak   AK10-9 48 Nm peak   18 Nm rated
Level walk (peak, Winter ~0.5 Nm/kg, UNVERIFIED)    65 Nm      74 %              28 %
Stair ascent (Riener 2002 via review)              138 Nm      35 %              13 %
Stair descent (Riener 2002 via review)             120 Nm      40 %              15 %
Run 3.5 m/s, midstance (Schache 2011)              407 Nm      12 %               4 %

Reading: walking and stairs can be covered well. Running gets ~13–18% of the
midstance knee moment from a 48 Nm peak — in the range other running exos target
(active running exos report 3.9–14.6% metabolic savings, review claim, UNVERIFIED).

=== CAN IT KEEP UP WITH A RUNNING KNEE? ===
Terminal swing:  -4.6 W/kg / 0.53 Nm/kg → ~8.7 rad/s (497°/s)
Initial stance:  -15.7 W/kg / 3.12 Nm/kg → ~5.0 rad/s
(A student lab report measured ~478°/s at an unstated speed — same ballpark, weak source.)
AK10-9 on a 21.6 V 6S bus: no-load ~25.1 rad/s; vendor rated speed 205 rpm = 21.5 rad/s at 24 V → 2.5x margin. OK.
AK80-64 rated 23 rpm = 2.4 rad/s → 28% of the swing speed. It would FIGHT the runner. Rejected.

Reflected rotor inertia: 1002 g·cm² x 9² = 0.0081 kg·m²
Shank+foot about the knee (median man): ~0.403 kg·m² → actuator adds 2.0%. Transparent enough.
Backdrive torque 0.8 Nm (vendor) — the wearer feels this when unpowered.

=== FALL CATCH — what it can and cannot do ===
87.4 kg: a hard single-leg catch is running-class load ~273 Nm; one AK10-9 gives 48 Nm = 18%.
130.3 kg: a hard single-leg catch is running-class load ~407 Nm; one AK10-9 gives 48 Nm = 12%.
So "fall catch" = both knees go to peak torque + high damping in a few ms to BUY TIME and
keep the knee from buckling fast. It does NOT hold a falling adult. The tooth lock must
never be thrown at speed — dropping a pawl into a moving ring is how teeth break.

=== BATTERY: 2 x 6S1P Molicel P45B = 2 x 97.2 Wh = 194 Wh; 165 Wh usable (85% EST); ~1.04 kg ===
Each pack is under the FAA 100 Wh carry-on line. Runner/hiker: median US man, 87.4 kg.

--- RUNNING, 3.5 m/s ---
Assist: 48 Nm peak per knee = 17.6% of the biological midstance moment (capped by actuator peak)
Per stride per leg: copper 11.7 J + positive 6.3 J/0.85 − regen 20.6 J x 0.85 = 1.6 J
Strategy A — MIRROR (exo torque follows the whole knee moment, brakes AND pushes):
  no regen credit: 58 W → 2.8 h (36 km)   ← HEADLINE (conservative)
  with regen:      9 W → 17.7 h. The knee is a net ABSORBER in running (−1.34 vs +0.41 J/kg per stride, Schache),
                   so on paper braking recovers ~49 W. NOT CLAIMED until a bench shows it — eta_in is a guess.
Strategy B — PUSH-ONLY (torque only in the 0.12 s terminal-stance push, EST): 41 W → 4.0 h (51 km)
Whether ANY knee assist lowers a runner's metabolic cost is UNPROVEN (Elliott/Herr clutch-spring exo did not).

--- HIKING, 10% grade, ~5 km/h (Tobler flat speed; uphill is slower — runtime is per hour of assist) ---
assist 15 Nm pk: UP  22 W →  7.4 h | exo lifts 10 W of 118 W climbing power (9%) | DOWN   1.5 W (regen covers part of idle) → 112.3 h
assist 25 Nm pk: UP  40 W →  4.2 h | exo lifts 17 W of 118 W climbing power (15%) | DOWN   4.9 W (regen covers part of idle) → 33.5 h
assist 35 Nm pk: UP  62 W →  2.7 h | exo lifts 24 W of 118 W climbing power (20%) | DOWN  13.1 W (regen < copper) → 12.7 h

Downhill regen at 25 Nm: 14.6 W harvested before copper loss; net to battery 0.1 W.
Cross-check: Donelan 2008 knee harvester measured 4.8 ± 0.8 W walking at 1.5 m/s; Wu 2022 reports ~6.5 W downhill per leg (UNVERIFIED convention).
Best NET regen: brake at 12.5 Nm peak → 3.7 W to the battery. Harder braking turns the extra into heat (copper ∝ T²).
One hour of descent at that setting banks ~3.7 Wh of 165 Wh usable (2.2%).
FINDING: a low-ratio QDD knee motor is a POOR generator at hiking speeds — copper loss eats most of the
harvest. Donelan's 4.8 W came from a purpose-built high-ratio generator. Downhill mode is worth having
because it takes braking load off the quads, NOT because it charges the battery. Say so in the README.

--- SITTING (pawl locked) ---
Lock holds mechanically; motors off. Draw = idle 5 W → 33 h. Sitting costs almost nothing.
(Holding the chair on motors instead would need >142 Nm per knee — impossible on this actuator; see seat_torque_model.mjs.)

--- A DAY HIKE: 3 h up at 25 Nm, 2 h down at 25 Nm, 4 x 15 min seat breaks ---
Energy: 119 + 10 + 5 = 134 Wh vs 165 Wh usable → FITS (81% of the packs).

All efficiencies, stride rates and the assist profile are EST. The bench (M1) replaces them.
