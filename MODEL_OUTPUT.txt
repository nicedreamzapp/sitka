=== SEAT MODE — knee torque to hold a 95th-percentile US man ===
Person: 130.3 kg (NHANES 95th), 1.874 m (NHANES 95th) — both marginals, paired on purpose
Thigh 0.459 m, shank 0.461 m (Winter fractions), pad CoP 0.298 m from knee (EST 65% of thigh)
Load per leg, both legs sharing, 100% of mass above the shanks: 566 N

Mode        knee  bal.shank  hip ht   M balanced  M worst   x1.5 drop  x2 one-leg  LOCK DESIGN (xSF1.5)
Perch         25°     9.8°   0.98 m      44 Nm     71 Nm     107 Nm      143 Nm     214 Nm
Half-sit      60°    23.0°   0.87 m     102 Nm    146 Nm     220 Nm      293 Nm     439 Nm
Chair         90°    32.9°   0.72 m     142 Nm    169 Nm     253 Nm      338 Nm     507 Nm
Deep squat   120°    35.0°*  0.50 m     168 Nm    169 Nm     253 Nm      338 Nm     507 Nm
  * shank lean capped at the EST ankle limit — the pad sits behind the heel, feet must take the rest

=== CAN THE MOTOR HOLD IT? ===
Chair, balanced, static: 142 Nm per knee.
CubeMars AK10-9 V2.0 KV100: 18 Nm rated / 48 Nm peak → 3.0x its PEAK. No.
CubeMars AK80-64 KV80: 48 Nm rated / 120 Nm peak → 3.0x its RATED. No — and at 23 rpm it cannot follow a running knee anyway.
Holding 48 Nm on the AK10-9 alone burns 92 W per knee as heat — minutes, not hours.
VERDICT: the seat is held by a MECHANICAL lock. The motor only positions the knee so the lock can drop in.

=== LOCK SIZING (worst mode: Chair, 507 Nm design) ===
Pawl on a toothed ring at r = 35 mm → tooth force 14.5 kN
17-4PH H900, shear = 0.6 x 1310 MPa (handbook, VERIFY) → shear area ≥ 18 mm²
SHEAR ONLY. Contact (Hertz) and tooth-bending checks are NOT done yet; plan on 3x this area until they are.
So: e.g. two pawl teeth, each 10 mm wide x 3 mm engaged. The lock itself is small.

=== THIGH UPRIGHT (the part that IS hard) ===
Design moment 507 Nm at yield 503 MPa (7075-T6, VERIFY) → section modulus ≥ 1008 mm³
One lateral 7075 bar 8 mm thick needs to be 28 mm deep → 0.28 kg per 450 mm thigh upright.
A single 25x22 mm roll-wrapped carbon tube (Z ≈ 614 mm³) would see 826 MPa — beyond what we can count on from a roll-wrapped tube. Carbon tubes must go bilateral or much bigger.

=== HOW MUCH OF THE KNEE CAN ONE AK10-9 CARRY? ===

-- median man, 87.4 kg --
Activity                                         bio peak   AK10-9 48 Nm peak   18 Nm rated
Level walk (peak, Winter ~0.5 Nm/kg, UNVERIFIED)    44 Nm     110 %              41 %
Stair ascent (Riener 2002 via review)               93 Nm      52 %              19 %
Stair descent (Riener 2002 via review)              80 Nm      60 %              22 %
Run 3.5 m/s, midstance (Schache 2011)              273 Nm      18 %               7 %

-- 95th pct man, 130.3 kg --
Activity                                         bio peak   AK10-9 48 Nm peak   18 Nm rated
Level walk (peak, Winter ~0.5 Nm/kg, UNVERIFIED)    65 Nm      74 %              28 %
Stair ascent (Riener 2002 via review)              138 Nm      35 %              13 %
Stair descent (Riener 2002 via review)             120 Nm      40 %              15 %
Run 3.5 m/s, midstance (Schache 2011)              407 Nm      12 %               4 %

Reading: walking and stairs can be covered well. Running gets ~13–18% of the
midstance knee moment from a 48 Nm peak — in the range other running exos target
(active running exos report 3.9–14.6% metabolic savings, review claim, UNVERIFIED).

=== CAN IT KEEP UP WITH A RUNNING KNEE? ===
Terminal swing:  -4.6 W/kg / 0.53 Nm/kg → ~8.7 rad/s (497°/s)
Initial stance:  -15.7 W/kg / 3.12 Nm/kg → ~5.0 rad/s
(A student lab report measured ~478°/s at an unstated speed — same ballpark, weak source.)
AK10-9 on a 21.6 V 6S bus: no-load ~25.1 rad/s; vendor rated speed 205 rpm = 21.5 rad/s at 24 V → 2.5x margin. OK.
AK80-64 rated 23 rpm = 2.4 rad/s → 28% of the swing speed. It would FIGHT the runner. Rejected.

Reflected rotor inertia: 1002 g·cm² x 9² = 0.0081 kg·m²
Shank+foot about the knee (median man): ~0.403 kg·m² → actuator adds 2.0%. Transparent enough.
Backdrive torque 0.8 Nm (vendor) — the wearer feels this when unpowered.

=== FALL CATCH — what it can and cannot do ===
87.4 kg: a hard single-leg catch is running-class load ~273 Nm; one AK10-9 gives 48 Nm = 18%.
130.3 kg: a hard single-leg catch is running-class load ~407 Nm; one AK10-9 gives 48 Nm = 12%.
So "fall catch" = both knees go to peak torque + high damping in a few ms to BUY TIME and
keep the knee from buckling fast. It does NOT hold a falling adult. The tooth lock must
never be thrown at speed — dropping a pawl into a moving ring is how teeth break.

# node energy_model.mjs
##############################################################################
=== BATTERY: 2 x 6S1P Molicel P45B = 2 x 97.2 Wh = 194 Wh; 165 Wh usable (85% EST); ~1.04 kg ===
Each pack is under the FAA 100 Wh carry-on line. Runner/hiker: median US man, 87.4 kg.

--- RUNNING, 3.5 m/s ---
Assist: 48 Nm peak per knee = 17.6% of the biological midstance moment (capped by actuator peak)
Per stride per leg: copper 11.7 J + positive 6.3 J/0.85 − regen 20.6 J x 0.85 = 1.6 J
Strategy A — MIRROR (exo torque follows the whole knee moment, brakes AND pushes):
  no regen credit: 58 W → 2.8 h (36 km)   ← HEADLINE (conservative)
  with regen:      9 W → 17.7 h. The knee is a net ABSORBER in running (−1.34 vs +0.41 J/kg per stride, Schache),
                   so on paper braking recovers ~49 W. NOT CLAIMED until a bench shows it — eta_in is a guess.
Strategy B — PUSH-ONLY (torque only in the 0.12 s terminal-stance push, EST): 41 W → 4.0 h (51 km)
Whether ANY knee assist lowers a runner's metabolic cost is UNPROVEN (Elliott/Herr clutch-spring exo did not).

--- HIKING, 10% grade, ~5 km/h (Tobler flat speed; uphill is slower — runtime is per hour of assist) ---
assist 15 Nm pk: UP  22 W →  7.4 h | exo lifts 10 W of 118 W climbing power (9%) | DOWN   1.5 W (regen covers part of idle) → 112.3 h
assist 25 Nm pk: UP  40 W →  4.2 h | exo lifts 17 W of 118 W climbing power (15%) | DOWN   4.9 W (regen covers part of idle) → 33.5 h
assist 35 Nm pk: UP  62 W →  2.7 h | exo lifts 24 W of 118 W climbing power (20%) | DOWN  13.1 W (regen < copper) → 12.7 h

Downhill regen at 25 Nm: 14.6 W harvested before copper loss; net to battery 0.1 W.
Cross-check: Donelan 2008 knee harvester measured 4.8 ± 0.8 W walking at 1.5 m/s; Wu 2022 reports ~6.5 W downhill per leg (UNVERIFIED convention).
Best NET regen: brake at 12.5 Nm peak → 3.7 W to the battery. Harder braking turns the extra into heat (copper ∝ T²).
One hour of descent at that setting banks ~3.7 Wh of 165 Wh usable (2.2%).
FINDING: a low-ratio QDD knee motor is a POOR generator at hiking speeds — copper loss eats most of the
harvest. Donelan's 4.8 W came from a purpose-built high-ratio generator. Downhill mode is worth having
because it takes braking load off the quads, NOT because it charges the battery. Say so in the README.

--- SITTING (pawl locked) ---
Lock holds mechanically; motors off. Draw = idle 5 W → 33 h. Sitting costs almost nothing.
(Holding the chair on motors instead would need >142 Nm per knee — impossible on this actuator; see seat_torque_model.mjs.)

--- A DAY HIKE: 3 h up at 25 Nm, 2 h down at 25 Nm, 4 x 15 min seat breaks ---
Energy: 119 + 10 + 5 = 134 Wh vs 165 Wh usable → FITS (81% of the packs).

All efficiencies, stride rates and the assist profile are EST. The bench (M1) replaces them.
